The corruption modes decide the errors of each signal region first

A flat receiver with an error model no longer decides the whole reception with one random draw and the SNIR of the whole reception, and the chunk, byte, and bit modes no longer sample the errors a second time until some error occurs. The decision of the header and the decision of the data decide each of their regions one time: a region fails if its SNIR is not above the SNIR threshold of the receiver, or else if one random draw against its error rate fails, both with the SNIR of the time of that region. A region is the whole part in the packet mode, and one chunk in the chunk, byte, and bit modes. The preamble needs only the SNIR threshold, and the data needs a correct header. The received packet gets the failed regions: a packet-level bit error in the packet mode, an incorrect copy of each failed chunk in the chunk mode, and a raw chunk with flipped bytes or bits, marked incorrect, in the byte and bit modes. In these three modes, only a failed header gives a packet-level bit error, so interference over the end of a frame marks only the late chunks incorrect. A reception in the packet mode draws up to two random numbers instead of one, so the results of the wireless simulations with reception errors change. ApskReceiver, Ieee802154NarrowbandReceiver, and Ieee80211Receiver support all four modes. IErrorModel::computeCorruptedPacket(snir) goes away.
